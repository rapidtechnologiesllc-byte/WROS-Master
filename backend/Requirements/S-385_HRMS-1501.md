## Why — Business Rationale

- A panel's hire/no-hire feedback is usually a reliable signal, but interviews are increasingly vulnerable to a specific failure mode a panel can't always detect in the moment — a candidate reading prepared answers, receiving real-time outside help, or someone other than the actual candidate answering. A panel that felt good about an interview has no independent way to check that feeling against what the recording actually shows. This engine exists to give them that second signal — explicitly framed from the start as a cross-check, not a verdict.

## What — Scope

- An analysis engine that processes an interview's recording or transcript and produces a structured integrity assessment — flagging patterns consistent with rehearsed/predetermined answers, indications of real-time external assistance, or signals suggesting the person answering may not match the registered candidate — with a confidence level and specific supporting evidence, not a bare accusation.

## Before — Prerequisites

- Interview recording or transcription capability must be provisioned and consented to — see Business Rules for the mandatory consent requirement, which is a prerequisite this story does not itself solve but strictly depends on.

- HRMS-0248 InterviewPanelAssigner must be live — this engine attaches to an existing scheduled interview, it doesn't create one.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Confirm consent gate before any recording is analyzed

- This engine only processes an interview recording/transcript if consent was captured from all parties at scheduling time (per the Business Rules below) — the engine itself checks this gate before doing any analysis, it does not assume upstream consent capture is reliable.

## Step 2: Build or integrate the transcription stage

- If the interview platform doesn't already provide a transcript, generate one via standard speech-to-text, timestamped.

## Step 3: Build the LLM analysis stage

- Construct the analysis prompt from the full timestamped transcript.

- Parse the structured output: concern level, categories, supporting evidence, confidence.

- Reject and retry once on malformed output; on second failure, mark the analysis INCOMPLETE — never guess at a concern level from a partial or failed parse.

## Step 4: Store the assessment, visible to no one until S-386's gate opens it

- The assessment is written to storage but is not surfaced anywhere — not to the panel, not to the recruiter — until S-386 explicitly releases it after the panel's own feedback is independently submitted.

## Business Rules

BR: BR-1501-01 — Interview recording/analysis requires explicit consent from all parties, captured before the interview, not inferred

- Analyzing someone's recorded interview for integrity signals is a meaningfully different thing than a standard recruiting interaction — every participant (candidate and panel) must be explicitly informed this analysis will occur and consent to being recorded/analyzed before the interview starts, matching platform-wide consent discipline applied to a higher-sensitivity use case.

- Field: recording_consent_given (boolean, all parties) must be true before this engine will process anything; the interview scheduling flow, not this story, is responsible for capturing that consent

BR: BR-1501-02 — This engine takes no action and is invisible to the panel until S-386 explicitly releases it

- The entire design intent is that the panel forms and submits their own independent judgment first — showing them the AI's assessment before they submit their own feedback would contaminate the very independent signal this system exists to cross-check against.

- Field: Assessment storage has no read path available to panel or recruiter roles until S-386's post-submission release gate opens it

BR: BR-1501-03 — A failed or incomplete analysis is marked INCOMPLETE, never defaulted to a concern level

- Guessing at an integrity concern from partial data risks a false signal about a real person's interview — an honest 'the analysis didn't complete' is safer than a confident-looking wrong answer.

- Field: Malformed LLM output after one retry sets integrity_concern_level=INCOMPLETE, not a default like NONE or LOW

BR: BR-1501-04 — Supporting evidence must be specific and timestamped, never a vague characterization

- A concern flag with no specific, checkable evidence behind it isn't a useful cross-check, it's just an unfalsifiable accusation — every flagged concern must point to something a human can independently verify by reviewing the specific moment in the transcript.

- Field: supporting_evidence[] entries each require a timestamp and a specific quoted or described pattern, enforced at output-parsing time — a concern with no evidence entries is rejected as malformed

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: No interview is analyzed without recorded consent from all parties, verified by attempting analysis on a non-consented interview and confirming it's blocked.

- AC-2: The assessment is not retrievable by any panel or recruiter role until S-386's release gate opens it, verified by direct API access attempt before panel feedback submission.

- AC-3: A malformed or failed LLM analysis results in INCOMPLETE status, never a guessed concern level.

- AC-4: Every HIGH or MEDIUM concern includes at least one timestamped, specific supporting evidence entry — an assessment with an elevated concern level but no evidence is rejected as invalid output.

## Not In Scope

- No real-time, in-interview flagging or intervention — this engine analyzes only after the interview concludes, it never interrupts or influences the interview itself.

- No automatic candidate rejection, status change, or any action of any kind based on this engine's output — analysis only, per BR-1501-02.

- No biometric identity verification (face matching, voice biometrics) in this story — the identity-mismatch signal is limited to conversational/speech-pattern analysis via the LLM, not a dedicated biometric system, which would be a separate, more heavily-scrutinized future story if ever pursued.
