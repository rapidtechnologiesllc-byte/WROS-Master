## Why — Business Rationale

- Every downstream financial number in this platform — invoices, revenue recognition, partner incentives — ultimately depends on a timesheet actually being submitted and approved on time. Chasing that manually, one person at a time, across every employee every week, is exactly the kind of mechanical follow-up work this platform exists to remove from a human's plate. This agent escalates through increasingly higher-visibility channels and increasingly senior people specifically so a chronically late submitter can't stay invisible to their own management chain.

## What — Scope

- A scheduled agent that tracks timesheet submission status against a Friday due date and escalates through a precise 7-step channel-and-recipient cascade — email, Teams, WhatsApp to the employee, then email/notification up the reporting chain to their manager, their manager's manager, and finally the BU Head — stopping the moment the timesheet is actually submitted, at whatever step that happens.

## Before — Prerequisites

- HRMS-1404 Dynamic Reporting Hierarchy Engine must be live — steps 6 and 7 of the cascade require walking the resolved reporting chain (manager's manager, then BU Head), not a hardcoded two-level lookup.

- HRMS-1401/1402/1403 (embedded Outlook/Teams) and WhatsApp Business API must be live for the channel-specific sends in steps 1-4.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build the cascade state machine

- One row per employee per week: due_date (Friday), current_escalation_step, last_escalation_sent_at, submitted (boolean), submitted_at.

- Step 1 (Friday, due date): email to employee.

- Step 2 (Monday, still pending): email to employee.

- Step 3 (Monday + 4 hours in the employee's local timezone per HRMS-0121, still pending): Teams message via HRMS-1403's embedded dock, to employee.

- Step 4 (Tuesday, still pending): WhatsApp to employee.

- Step 5 (Wednesday, still pending): email/notification to the employee's direct reporting manager, resolved via HRMS-1404.

- Step 6 (Thursday, still pending): email/notification to the reporting manager's own manager, one level further up the same resolved chain.

- Step 7 (still pending after Thursday): email/notification to the BU Head.

## Step 2: Wire the timezone-aware Step 3 trigger

- 4 hours is measured in the employee's own configured timezone (HRMS-0121), not a single global clock — an employee in a different continent shouldn't get an off-hours Teams ping because the calculation used someone else's business day.

## Step 3: Build the stop condition

- The moment `submitted=true` is detected (checked at the start of every scheduled run, not just at cascade trigger points), all further escalation for that employee/week stops immediately — no step fires after submission, even if it was already queued for that day.

## Step 4: Wire the reporting-chain walk for steps 6-7

- Steps 6 and 7 call HRMS-1404's hierarchy resolution live at the time of escalation, not a cached value from when the employee was hired — an employee whose manager changed mid-week correctly escalates up their current chain, not a stale one.

## Business Rules

BR: BR-1601-01 — The cascade never skips a step or terminates early except on actual submission

- Every escalation level is a deliberate visibility guarantee — a manager being notified at step 5 doesn't make step 6 optional if the timesheet is still outstanding the next day; the point is that non-submission becomes visible to progressively more senior people, not that one notification suffices.

- Field: Step progression is strictly sequential and time-driven; only submitted=true halts it

BR: BR-1601-02 — Step 3's 4-hour window is timezone-aware per employee, not a global clock

- A multi-continent workforce means '4 hours into Monday' means something different depending on where the employee actually is — using a single reference timezone would misfire for anyone outside it.

- Field: Step 3 trigger time is computed against HRMS-0121's per-employee timezone configuration

BR: BR-1601-03 — Steps 6 and 7 resolve the reporting chain live, not from a cached hierarchy snapshot

- A same-day reporting-line change (per HRMS-1404's own real-time recalculation trigger) must be reflected — escalating to someone's former manager because the chain was cached at hire time would defeat the purpose of building HRMS-1404 as a dynamic engine in the first place.

- Field: Steps 6-7 call HRMS-1404's resolution function at escalation time, never a stored/cached level

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: An employee who submits their timesheet at any point immediately halts all further escalation for that week, verified by submitting mid-cascade and confirming no further steps fire.

- AC-2: Step 3 fires exactly 4 hours into Monday in the employee's own configured timezone, not a global reference time, verified across two employees in different timezones.

- AC-3: Steps 6 and 7 correctly follow a same-day reporting-chain change, verified by changing an employee's manager mid-week and confirming escalation follows the new chain.

- AC-4: A fully non-responsive employee produces exactly the 7-step sequence in order, with no step skipped or duplicated.

## Not In Scope

- No automatic timesheet auto-submission or estimation on the employee's behalf — this agent nags, it never fills in or guesses at hours.

- No suppression or snoozing of the cascade by the employee themselves — only actual submission stops it.
