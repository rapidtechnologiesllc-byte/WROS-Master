## Why — Business Rationale

- S-385's assessment is worthless sitting unused in a database — but surfacing it the wrong way risks exactly what Riya was explicit about avoiding: an AI overriding a panel's judgment. This story is the deliberate, narrow bridge between the two — compare what the panel decided against what the engine flagged, and when they meaningfully disagree, ask the panel a clarifying question instead of declaring either one right.

## What — Scope

- A comparison step that runs after a panel submits their interview outcome (per HRMS-0248's existing feedback flow) — if S-385's assessment shows a MEDIUM or HIGH integrity concern on an interview the panel marked Hire/Pass, the panel is asked specific clarifying questions grounded in the flagged evidence, with their response logged alongside the original decision. The panel's decision is never changed, blocked, or overridden by this process.

## Before — Prerequisites

- S-385 must be live and producing assessments.

- HRMS-0248's existing panel feedback submission flow must be the trigger point this story hooks into.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build the post-submission comparison check

- Triggered the moment a panel submits Hire/Pass feedback (the concerning direction — a Reject/Fail feedback combined with a HIGH integrity concern is lower-stakes and does not trigger clarification, since both signals already point the same direction).

- Compare S-385's integrity_concern_level for that interview against the panel's submitted decision.

## Step 2: Define the disagreement threshold

- MEDIUM or HIGH concern level combined with a Hire/Pass decision triggers clarification routing.

- LOW or NONE concern, or an INCOMPLETE assessment, never triggers anything — this story only acts on a meaningful, evidenced disagreement.

## Step 3: Build the clarification request

- Present the panel with S-385's specific supporting evidence (timestamped, concrete) and ask targeted, non-accusatory clarifying questions — e.g., 'The system flagged an unusually long pause before this specific technical answer at 14:32 — did you notice anything unusual at that point?' — never a bare 'the AI thinks this candidate cheated.'

## Step 4: Log the panel's response without altering the original decision

- Whatever the panel says — confirms the concern, explains it away, or stands firm — their original Hire/Pass decision remains completely unchanged and continues through the normal pipeline (subject to R-05's existing L1-before-L2 gating and every other existing rule, untouched by this story).

- The full exchange (original decision, S-385's assessment, panel's clarification response) is logged permanently for BU Head/Director visibility as a pattern-tracking signal over time, not for reversing this specific decision.

## Business Rules

BR: BR-1502-01 — This process never changes, blocks, or overrides the panel's decision, under any circumstance

- This is the explicit, non-negotiable design constraint this entire epic exists within — the system's role is limited to asking a grounded clarifying question and logging the answer, full stop.

- Field: No code path in this story writes to the interview outcome/decision field — it is read-only with respect to that field throughout

BR: BR-1502-02 — Clarification only triggers on a MEDIUM/HIGH concern paired with a Hire/Pass decision

- A Reject/Fail decision alongside a flagged concern doesn't need reconciling — both signals already agree, so asking the panel to justify a decision the AI didn't disagree with would be noise, not signal.

- Field: Trigger logic requires both conditions simultaneously: concern_level IN (MEDIUM, HIGH) AND decision IN (Hire, Pass)

BR: BR-1502-03 — Clarifying questions must reference specific evidence, never a generic suspicion

- A vague 'the AI is concerned about this interview' is unfalsifiable and puts the panel in an unfair position — every clarification prompt must point to a specific timestamped pattern the panel can actually recall or check.

- Field: Clarification prompt text is generated directly from S-385's supporting_evidence array, never a freeform generic message

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: A Hire decision paired with a HIGH integrity concern triggers a clarification request grounded in specific evidence.

- AC-2: A Reject decision paired with a HIGH integrity concern does not trigger any clarification request.

- AC-3: The panel's original decision is provably unchanged after a clarification exchange, verified by comparing the decision field before and after.

- AC-4: A clarification prompt always references at least one specific, timestamped piece of evidence from S-385, never generic language.

## Not In Scope

- No automatic decision reversal, hold, or escalation to a different decision-maker based on the panel's clarification response — the response is logged for pattern visibility only.

- No panel performance scoring or penalty tied to how they respond to a clarification request — this must never feel like the panel itself is being tested or graded, or panels will start second-guessing genuine hire decisions defensively.
