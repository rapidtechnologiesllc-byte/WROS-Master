## Why — Business Rationale

- A rejected timesheet with nowhere for the employee to actually fix it just sits stuck, blocking the invoice per R-10. And a client whose approvals are consistently slow is a revenue-recognition risk nobody notices until finance asks why an invoice is three weeks late. The correction loop, the historical record clients need for their own finance teams, and the SLA watch that catches slow approvers before they become a pattern were three separate stories, but they're one lifecycle: submit, approve or reject-and-fix, then the closed record needs to be visible and the whole loop needs a clock on it.

## What — Scope

- The full timesheet correction loop — employee corrects only the flagged entries and resubmits, capped at 3 attempts before CS intervenes — plus a client-facing approval history/export view and a backend SLA monitor that alerts CS when a client's approval is pending past 5 business days.

## Before — Prerequisites

- S-182 Client Timesheet Approval Portal View must be live — correction and SLA monitoring both extend that base workflow.

- S-183 Reassign Approver must be live so SLA tracking correctly follows a reassigned approver, not the original.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build the correction flow

- On client Reject with comments, the specific flagged daily entries (not the whole timesheet) are reopened for employee edit.

- Employee corrects only flagged entries, adds a response note, resubmits.

- resubmission_count increments; 3rd rejection auto-escalates to BlitzenX CS for direct intervention rather than a 4th employee attempt.

## Step 2: Build the approval history & export view

- Client views all approved timesheets filterable by month, employee, project — scoped strictly to their own company per Section 12.

- CSV and PDF export for the client's own finance team reconciliation.

## Step 3: Build the SLA monitor

- Tracks days_pending per timesheet from submission to approval.

- Day 3: reminder notification to the client approver (or their reassigned substitute per S-183).

- Day 5 (business days): escalation alert to BlitzenX CS — this is the SLA breach point.

- An unapproved timesheet blocks invoice generation for that period per R-10 — this monitor is what gives CS visibility before that block becomes a client-facing invoicing delay.

## Business Rules

## BR: BR-0716-01 — Correction only reopens the specifically flagged entries

- An employee cannot silently edit unflagged, already-implicitly-accepted entries during a correction pass — only what the client explicitly rejected is editable.

- Field: Correction UI enforces field-level edit lock on all entries except those in the rejection's flagged set

## BR: BR-0716-02 — Maximum 3 resubmission attempts, then mandatory CS intervention

- Prevents an unresolvable back-and-forth between employee and client from stalling indefinitely — a 3rd rejection is a signal something needs a human mediator, not a 4th automated attempt.

- Field: resubmission_count >= 3 blocks further employee resubmission, routes to CS queue

## BR: BR-0716-03 — Unapproved timesheets block invoice generation per R-10

- Directly enforces the platform-wide R-10 rule — this story's SLA monitor exists specifically to surface that blocking risk to CS before it becomes an invoicing delay discovered too late.

- Field: HRMS-0605 TimesheetRevenueProcessor checks timesheet approval status before generating any invoice line

BR: BR-0716-04 — Approval history export is scoped strictly to the requesting client's own company

- A client finance user must never be able to export or view another client's timesheet data, even accidentally.

- Field: Export query filtered by client_id from session, same scoping discipline as HRMS-0109

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: A correction pass only allows editing of the specific entries flagged in the client's rejection.

- AC-2: A 3rd rejection on the same timesheet auto-routes to CS and blocks a 4th employee resubmission attempt.

- AC-3: A timesheet pending client approval past 5 business days triggers a CS escalation alert.

- AC-4: A client's approval history export contains only their own company's timesheets, verified with a cross-client test.

## Not In Scope

- No AI-assisted correction suggestions — the employee corrects based on the client's stated feedback, not an automated guess.

- No automatic timesheet re-approval — every resubmission requires explicit client re-review, even for a trivial correction.
