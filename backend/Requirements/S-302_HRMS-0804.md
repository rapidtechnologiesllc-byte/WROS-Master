## Why — Business Rationale

- EPIC-11's Project Health Monitoring Agent weighs milestone status at 35% of its composite health score — that weighting is meaningless without a real milestone tracking system underneath it. This story is that foundation: the actual due dates, completion states, and delay tracking the agent reads from.

## What — Scope

- Project milestone definition with due dates, completion marking, delay tracking, a visual timeline, and timezone-aware date handling for multi-continent teams.

## Before — Prerequisites

- S-299 must be live — milestones belong to an existing project.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build milestone creation

- Title, due date, description, owner — due dates stored and displayed timezone-aware per HRMS-0121's locale config, since a project team spanning continents needs a due date that means the same moment for everyone.

## Step 2: Build completion marking

- Mark complete with an actual completion date — if past due_date, delay is automatically computed, not manually entered.

## Step 3: Build the visual timeline

- Gantt-style or simple chronological view of all milestones for a project, overdue ones visually flagged.

## Business Rules

## BR: BR-0804-01 — Delay is always computed, never manually entered

- A milestone's delay-in-days must be an objective fact derived from due_date and completion_date, not something that could be fudged by manual entry — this is exactly the data HRMS-1107's health score depends on being trustworthy.

- Field: delay_days = completion_date - due_date, computed at completion time, no manual override field exists

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: A milestone completed after its due date automatically computes and stores the correct delay in days.

- AC-2: Due dates display correctly in each viewer's own configured timezone for the same underlying stored moment.

- AC-3: HRMS-1107 can successfully read milestone status and delay data from this table.
