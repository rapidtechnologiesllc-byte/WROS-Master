## 2. WHY THIS EXISTS

BlitzenX sources candidates from LinkedIn scraping, WhatsApp inbound, email inbound, sub-vendor submissions, and direct recruiter entry. Understanding which source produces the best candidates (highest quality scores, fastest time-to-hire, highest offer acceptance rate) enables better allocation of sourcing effort and budget.

Source attribution tracks the full journey: a candidate may enter as a LinkedIn skeleton, get contact info from InMail, and be engaged by Thunder via WhatsApp. The attribution chain shows all of this.

## 3. WHAT YOU ARE BUILDING

Build: (1) source attribution chain on each candidate (all channels touched, in order). (2) Attribution API endpoint. (3) Source summary on candidate profile. (4) Feed source data to HRMS-1208 analytics.

## 5. STEP-BY-STEP EXECUTION

▶  Follow these steps in exact order. If a step is unclear, ask the lead BA — never assume.

## Step 1: Build attribution chain from candidate_identity_events

- Attribution chain = ordered list of channels this candidate touched, from first contact to latest

- SELECT * FROM candidate_identity_events WHERE candidate_id=X AND tenant_id=Y ORDER BY created_at ASC

- Chain example: [LINKEDIN (skeleton Jan 1) → WHATSAPP (first reply Jan 3) → EMAIL (resume sent Jan 5) → PORTAL (offer viewed Jan 20)]

- First touch = candidates.source (set at creation). All subsequent touches = subsequent candidate_identity_events.

## Step 2: Build GET /api/candidates/{id}/attribution endpoint

- Auth: recruiter auth. Filter by tenant_id.

- Response: { first_touch_channel, first_touch_at, attribution_chain: [{channel, contact_identifier, event_type, created_at}], days_from_first_touch_to_qualified, days_from_first_touch_to_offer }

## Step 3: Show source attribution on candidate profile

- On candidate profile PROFILE tab: small 'Source' section showing: 'First contact: LinkedIn (Jan 1) → WhatsApp (Jan 3) → Email (Jan 5)'

- Rendered as horizontal chain with channel icons and dates
