## Why — Business Rationale

- The HTD program is a real investment — training time, buddy time, a slower path to billable work — and whether that investment is paying off compared to hiring laterally is exactly the kind of question that decides whether the program expands, holds steady, or gets cut. Without a direct comparison, that decision is made on intuition instead of evidence.

## What — Scope

- A comparison report between HTD-track and standard-track hires on cost per hire, time to deploy, revenue per head, and 12-month retention — the evidence base for justifying (or reconsidering) program expansion.

## Before — Prerequisites

- S-264 must be producing pool-eligible HTD graduates with a full journey history to compare against.

- S-362 HTD Pipeline Dashboard (REVISED) must be live for consistent conversion-rate context.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build the cost-per-hire comparison

- HTD: sourcing cost + training program cost + buddy time cost, amortized. Standard: sourcing and onboarding cost only. Both normalized to USD.

## Step 2: Build the time-to-deploy comparison

- HTD: from hire date to pool_eligible per S-264. Standard: from hire date to Specialty deployment per the standard Buddy Program gate.

## Step 3: Build the revenue-per-head comparison

- First-year billable revenue generated per hire, both tracks.

## Step 4: Build the 12-month retention comparison

- Percentage still active at BlitzenX 12 months post-deployment, both tracks.

## Business Rules

## BR: BR-P510-01 — Cost-per-hire for HTD includes amortized training and buddy time cost

- A fair comparison against standard hiring requires including the real cost of the training investment, not just recruiting spend, or HTD would look artificially cheap.

- Field: htd_cost_per_hire = sourcing_cost + training_program_cost + buddy_time_cost, all amortized per graduate
