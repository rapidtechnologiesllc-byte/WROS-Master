## Why — Business Rationale

- A project's health isn't only about milestones and staffing — it's also whether the engagement is actually profitable at the rates and cost structure currently assigned. A rough margin indicator gives a BU Head an early read on that without needing full finance-team involvement for every project.

## What — Scope

- Expected revenue computed from assigned roles' billing rates in the project's currency, with a rough margin indicator comparing that revenue against assigned resources' cost.

## Before — Prerequisites

- S-301 must be live — margin calculation requires actual resource assignments, not just planned roles.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build the expected revenue calculation

- Sum of each assigned resource's billing rate × allocation % × remaining project duration, in the project's currency.

## Step 2: Build the rough margin indicator

- expected_revenue minus an estimated resource cost (using each employee's fully-loaded cost, if available from the resource management data) — presented as a rough indicator, not a finance-grade margin calculation.

## Business Rules

BR: BR-0806-01 — This is explicitly labeled a rough indicator, not a finance-grade margin calculation

- Resource cost data used here may not capture every real cost component (e.g., full benefits allocation) — the UI must make clear this is directional, preventing it from being mistaken for an authoritative finance figure.

- Field: UI displays a persistent 'Estimate only — not a finance figure' label alongside the margin indicator

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: Expected revenue accurately reflects the sum of currently assigned resources' billing rate × allocation × remaining duration.

- AC-2: The margin indicator is always accompanied by its 'estimate only' disclosure.

- AC-3: Missing cost data for an assigned resource results in an explicit 'insufficient data' state, not a silently wrong margin.
