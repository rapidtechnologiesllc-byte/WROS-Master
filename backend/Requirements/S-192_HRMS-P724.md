## Why — Business Rationale

- Once an RFP's requirement is clear, the clock starts — a client who submitted a requirement expects to see progress within days, not weeks. This story is what makes sure sourcing effort begins the moment it's safe to begin, and — just as importantly — hands off to a human quickly when the AI genuinely can't find a match, rather than quietly stalling.

## What — Scope

- An AI sourcing agent assigned automatically the moment an RFP's Demand is created and clarifications are resolved — runs bench-first, then internal DB Boolean search, then LinkedIn, with a 48-hour target to produce submission-ready candidates, and a defined handoff package to a human recruiter when it can't.

## Before — Prerequisites

- S-187 must have produced a Demand record.

- S-191's clarification_complete gate must be checked before any sourcing action, per BR-0723-01.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Wire the trigger

- Listen for demand.created events originating from an RFP, gated on clarification_complete.

## Step 2: Run the bench-first check

- Call BenchFirstPolicyEngine.check() per R-04 — no external sourcing step begins before this returns insufficient.

## Step 3: Run the escalating search sequence

- Bench pool match, then internal DB Boolean search, then LinkedIn — each step logged with its result before proceeding to the next.

## Step 4: Evaluate handoff triggers

- At 48 hours or any other handoff condition, stop autonomous sourcing and assemble the handoff package.

## Step 5: Route to a human recruiter

- Assign to the appropriate recruiter with the full handoff package attached, flagged NEEDS_HUMAN.

## Business Rules

BR: BR-0724-01 — Bench-first is checked before any external sourcing step, no RFP-specific bypass

- R-04 applies uniformly regardless of demand origin — an RFP-sourced demand gets exactly the same bench-first discipline as any other.

- Field: BenchFirstPolicyEngine.check() call is mandatory before the internal-DB or LinkedIn search steps

BR: BR-0724-02 — Critical priority always hands off to a human, regardless of AI match success

- The stakes of a Critical-priority requirement going wrong are high enough that a human reviews it even if the AI believes it found a good match.

- Field: priority=Critical is an unconditional handoff trigger, independent of the 48-hour or quality thresholds

BR: BR-0724-03 — The handoff package must include the client's historical rejection pattern

- A recruiter picking up a handed-off demand shouldn't have to rediscover what this client has already rejected before — S-178's pattern data is included by default.

- Field: Handoff package assembly queries S-178 rejection-reason history for the same client_id

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: No external (internal-DB or LinkedIn) search step occurs before BenchFirstPolicyEngine.check() has run for that demand.

- AC-2: A Critical-priority RFP-originated demand hands off to a human recruiter regardless of whether the AI found a candidate match.

- AC-3: A demand with no match after 48 hours automatically hands off with a complete package, not a bare notification.

- AC-4: The handoff package includes the client's rejection-reason history where one exists.

## Not In Scope

- No autonomous candidate submission to the client — AI sourcing only prepares a shortlist; actual submission to the client remains a recruiter/CS action.

- No bypass of R-04 bench-first under any RFP priority level, including Critical — priority affects the handoff decision, not the bench-first requirement.
