## Why — Business Rationale

- A new hire's fully-loaded cost is a real, ongoing financial commitment, and your two-gate model — both a point-in-time reserve check and a trailing-run-rate check — exists specifically so a hiring decision doesn't get made on a single good month's numbers alone. Both gates have to pass, not either one, which is exactly the kind of hard financial discipline that's easy to talk about and easy to accidentally soften into 'mostly affordable' if it's not built as a genuinely hard gate.

## What — Scope

- A two-gate affordability check — Gate 1 (Reserve Balance exceeds the candidate's annual total cost) and Gate 2 (average of the trailing 3 months' NPBR, annualized, exceeds the candidate's annual total cost) — both required to pass, with clear headroom-or-shortfall reporting either way.

## Before — Prerequisites

- HRMS-1612 must be live for the Reserve Balance figure.

- HRMS-1607 must be live and have at least 3 months of NPBR history for the run-rate gate to be meaningful.

## Implementation Steps

## Step 1: Build Gate 1 — Reserve Check

- Required: Location Reserve Balance (per HRMS-1612, entity-specific to wherever the candidate would be hired) > Candidate Annual Total Cost.

- Pass/fail is binary, no partial credit — a Reserve Balance $1 short of the candidate's cost is a fail, matching your explicit "hard gate, no exceptions" framing.

## Step 2: Build Gate 2 — Run-Rate Check

- Average of the trailing 3 months' NPBR (before Reserve Contribution, per your formula) × 12, compared against Candidate Annual Total Cost.

- If fewer than 3 months of NPBR history exist yet (early in the platform's life), this gate cannot produce a meaningful answer — explicitly flag as "insufficient history" rather than defaulting to pass or fail on incomplete data.

## Step 3: Build the result logic

- Both PASS → HIRE APPROVED, with headroom shown (how much reserve/run-rate margin exists beyond the requirement).

- Either or both FAIL → NO HIRE, with the specific shortfall amount shown for whichever gate(s) failed, so the decision-maker knows exactly what would need to change.

## Business Rules

BR: BR-1613-01 — Both gates must independently pass; there is no override or partial-pass path

- Matches your explicit "hard gate, no exceptions" framing for both checks — this is one of the few places in the entire platform where even a BU Head logged justification does not create an exception, unlike R-04's bench-first override pattern.

- Field: Overall result is AND(gate1_pass, gate2_pass) with no override field anywhere in the schema

BR: BR-1613-02 — Gate 2 with fewer than 3 months of history reports INSUFFICIENT HISTORY, never a guessed pass or fail

- A platform newly live for BlitzenX won't have 3 months of NPBR data yet — defaulting this gate to an assumed pass would undermine the entire discipline the two-gate model exists to enforce.

- Field: Gate 2 requires nppr_history_months >= 3 before producing PASS/FAIL; otherwise returns INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY explicitly

## Acceptance Criteria

- AC-1: A candidate whose cost exceeds the Reserve Balance by even $1 fails Gate 1, with no override path anywhere in the system.

- AC-2: A check run with fewer than 3 months of NPBR history returns INSUFFICIENT HISTORY for Gate 2, never a defaulted pass or fail.

- AC-3: The overall result requires both gates to independently pass — verified with a test case where Gate 1 passes and Gate 2 fails, confirming the overall result is still NO HIRE.
