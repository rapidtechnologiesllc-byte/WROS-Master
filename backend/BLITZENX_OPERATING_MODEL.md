# 1. PURPOSE

BlitzenX is being designed to operate as an institutional company—not a founder-dependent company.

The objective is not simply to grow revenue or headcount.

The objective is to build an organization that can:

- Grow rapidly.
- Maintain quality.
- Generate predictable cash.
- Maintain strong EBITDA.
- Develop leaders.
- Institutionalize client relationships.
- Preserve organizational knowledge.
- Deploy talent intelligently.
- Reduce founder dependency.
- Use AI and WROS to automate management decisions.
- Scale toward **1,500 employees by 2030**.
- Become significantly more valuable than a traditional services company.

The fundamental test is:

> **If the CEO is unavailable for 30 days, the business should continue operating, selling, delivering, hiring, managing clients and making normal operating decisions without slowing down.**

The CEO should be the **top-line strategic executor**, not the day-to-day operating system.

# 2. THE BLITZENX MODEL

BlitzenX has two legal entities:

### BXUS
**BlitzenX US**

### BXIN
**BlitzenX India**

These are separate legal entities with an economic relationship.

BXIN operates as a subsidiary of BXUS.

The entities are economically connected, but the operating architecture is designed so that BXIN becomes increasingly economically independent.

The long-term objective is:

> **BXIN should generate enough independent economic value to cover 125% of its total organizational cost through SPECIALTY revenue and ultimately operate at approximately 20% net profit.**

BXUS should therefore progressively reduce its dependence on paying for offshore capacity.

The desired direction is:

**BXUS → CORE demand**

while:

**BXIN → SPECIALTY monetization + CORE delivery**

creates an increasingly self-sustaining offshore organization.

# 4. THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE

## THERE SHOULD NEVER BE CROSS-BU OPERATIONAL DEPENDENCY.

AXION and PRISM are independent businesses inside BlitzenX.

If AXION wins a client, AXION owns the responsibility to fulfill that business.

If PRISM wins a client, PRISM owns the responsibility to fulfill that business.

A BU cannot solve its workforce problem by reaching into another BU and borrowing its people.

If AXION needs capability:

> **AXION must build it.**

If PRISM needs capability:

> **PRISM must build it.**

The solution can be:

- Hire.
- Train.
- Develop.
- Redeploy within the BU.
- Automate.
- Partner.
- Build capability.

But the default answer is **not borrowing another BU's workforce.**

Cross-BU dependency creates:

- Ambiguous accountability.
- Political behavior.
- Resource conflicts.
- Weak BU ownership.
- Artificial profitability.
- Management dependency.
- Poor forecasting.

That is unacceptable.

# 8. CLIENT VS RESOURCE

This is a foundational rule.

> **The client belongs to the commercial owner. The employee belongs to the BU.**

These are two separate concepts.

A client is commercially owned by the Principal or appropriate commercial owner who won and owns the relationship.

The employee remains part of the BU organization.

### Example

If Troy wins a client:

> Client → Troy / AXION

The resources deployed to that client remain:

> AXION employees.

If Curtis wins a client:

> Client → Curtis / PRISM

The resources remain:

> PRISM employees.

The client does not become the property of the employee.

The employee does not become transferable inventory between BUs.

# 20. HTD TIMELINE

HTD employees:

- Spend approximately 90 days in training.
- Enter SPECIALTY after training.
- Remain in SPECIALTY for the development period.
- Reach CORE eligibility after approximately **365 days from the beginning of deployment**, subject to WROS certification and performance criteria.

HTD failure results in termination.

The objective is not to carry weak talent indefinitely.

---

# 21. LATERAL TALENT MODEL

Lateral Guidewire talent follows a different path.

Lateral talent must:

WROS determines when the individual is CORE-certified.

The BU Head receives a task to validate and confirm based on the system-generated evidence.
